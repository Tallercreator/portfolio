## Context and problem

The module was new — before it there was no transparency at all. Salary arrived as a single figure and nobody could see how it was reached.

Bonuses were the worst of it. People didn't know their own percentage, couldn't work out what it came to, and had no idea what it added up to over a year. The questions went into email threads — and every one of them meant somebody was making decisions about their own money blind.

Benefits were a separate story. Health insurance and the gym allowance existed, but activating them meant a paper process in Lotus. Awkward enough that some people simply never finished it.

## Inputs

This was the most complex module I've run. What was needed wasn't a picture with a number on it but a working formula: the components, the order of calculation, and the legal wording without which you can't show people sums at all.

I ran the research separately for web and mobile. Mobile is harder in principle: a formula that reads as one line on a wide screen doesn't fit on a phone.

What the research showed:

- people followed the calculations themselves, but the wording stayed heavy;
- they asked for the formula laid out horizontally as an alternative view.

Otherwise the launch went quietly.

## The decision

<Note kind="fork">
Show the whole calculation, or stop at the final figure with an explanation.

The second is easier in every way: fewer screens, less to agree with legal, no risk of somebody misreading the formula. But then the module doesn't do its job — it leaves the employee with a number again, and no way to check it.

I chose to show everything: the formula, all its components and the legal wording. The price of that choice turned up in the research — the wording, below.
</Note>

## Solution

Three parts tied together by one logic.

**Income structure** — what the payment is made of. **Calculation** — the formula with its components, including the bonus percentage and how it turns into a sum. **Benefits** — health insurance and gym activation, entirely inside the employee account.

I designed the mobile version from the start rather than adapting it later. People check their pay on a phone, and a formula is about the most awkward thing you can ask a small screen to display.

Activation isn't simple in itself — there are several conditions and steps. I broke it into steps and distributed the content so that at each one it's clear what is happening now and what comes next. Paperwork and Lotus left the process entirely.

## Outcome and reflection

CSI 4.7 / 5 on the first measurement, 412 respondents. Insurance and gym activation moved into the employee account: no paperwork, no Lotus.

Both research findings made it into the release. The horizontal layout of the formula shipped as an alternative to the vertical one, mainly for mobile, where the formula doesn't fit on a line.

The wording was rewritten by three of us: a lawyer, a CX researcher and me. Three, because none of us could do it alone. A legally precise sentence came out unreadable, a readable one lost precision, and both were equally unfit for a screen showing someone their own money. While we were at it we brought the copy across the section to one tone.
